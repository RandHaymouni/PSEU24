As I prepare for my 5-day trip to Barcelona from Queen Alia International Airport in Amman, Jordan, 
I’ve carefully selected options that balance both cost-effectiveness and convenience to ensure a smooth and enjoyable experience.

1. Flight
Airline: Royal Jordanian Airlines (Direct Flight)

I’ve chosen Royal Jordanian because it offers direct flights to Barcelona, which significantly reduces travel time.
A direct flight is always my preferred option, especially for a shorter trip, 
as it means less hassle and more time to explore the city. The flight is affordable if booked early, and the service is reliable.

2. Accommodation
Hotel: Ibis Barcelona Centro (Near La Rambla)
For accommodation, I’m staying at Ibis Barcelona Centro. 
I like this hotel for its ideal location in the heart of the city, 
close to many attractions like La Rambla and the Gothic Quarter. 
The hotel is known for being budget-friendly without compromising on comfort. 
I chose it because it offers good value for money, free Wi-Fi, and modern rooms—all of which are perfect for a solo traveler or anyone looking for a simple, central place to stay.

3. Transportation
Option: Barcelona Travel Card (T-10 Ticket)
To get around the city, I’ve opted for the T-10 Ticket. 
This allows me to travel across the metro, buses, and trams.
It’s perfect for my 5-day trip because I’ll only need 10 rides, and it’s much more affordable than paying for each ride individually.
For airport transfers, I’ll take the Aerobus—it’s quick, convenient, and very cost-effective, getting me directly from the airport to the city center.

4. Places to Visit
Day 1: La Sagrada Família
Visiting La Sagrada Família is a must for me. 
The architectural masterpiece by Antoni Gaudí is world-famous, and I’ve heard it’s even more spectacular in person. I’ll make sure to book my tickets in advance to avoid long lines.
Day 2: Park Güell
On my second day, I’ll visit Park Güell—another Gaudí creation. 
The park’s colorful mosaics and panoramic views of Barcelona are some of the highlights of the city. While I’ll pay to enter the Monumental Zone, the rest of the park is free to explore.
Day 3: Gothic Quarter & La Rambla
I love walking through La Rambla and the Gothic Quarter, 
where I can explore narrow streets full of history and discover local cafes, shops, and street performances. I’ll stop by Mercado de la Boqueria for fresh fruit and local snacks.
Day 4: Montjuïc Hill & Magic Fountain Show
I plan to take the cable car up Montjuïc Hill for stunning views of Barcelona. 
In the evening, I’ll enjoy the Magic Fountain Show—a light, music, and water show that’s free to watch.
Day 5: Picasso Museum & Beach Day
On the last day, I’ll visit the Picasso Museum to see some of his early works. 
Afterward, I’ll head to Barceloneta Beach to relax and enjoy the Mediterranean sun.

5. Food
For breakfast, I’ll enjoy pastries at local cafés like Brunch & Cake.
For lunch and dinner, I plan to try authentic paella and tapas at Can Culleretes.
If I need a budget option, I’ll head to El Raval, where I can find delicious and affordable meals.


Estimated Costs:
Flights: $500–$600 for a round-trip with Royal Jordanian.
Accommodation: $90–$120 per night at Ibis Barcelona Centro.
Transportation: $15 for the Barcelona Travel Card.
Attractions: Around $50–$70 for all the main attractions.
Food: $20–$30 per day.
